OLS was the fiscal sponsor for the 2026 Open Science Retreat in Machynlleth, Wales, organised by the Digital Research Academy. [Joyce Kao](https://digital-research.academy/about/) told us about this year's retreat, her experience with OLS fiscal sponsorship, and how to get involved in 2027!

### Tell us about yourself: how long have you been involved with the Open Science Retreat and the Digital Rights Academy? What is your role?

I’m Joyce. 
I’m one of the co-founders and co-executive directors of DRA. 
I was there in 2023 at the inaugural Open Science Retreat as well as the 2024 OS Retreat in the Netherlands as a participant. 
I orchestrated/co-organized the 2025 OS Retreat in Switzerland (also with OLS, [OILS](https://www.openinnovationlifesciences.com/), and  [UZH CRS](https://www.crs.uzh.ch/en.html)). 
Every year I run the Slide Karaoke Competition social event at the retreat where participants submit random slides and then other participants present the slides within 30 seconds of seeing them. The audience votes for the Slide Karaoke Champion of the year.

### Tell us about the Open Science Retreat and the Digital Research Academy

The [Open Science Retreat](https://open.science-retreat.org/) (OSR) and [Digital Research Academy](https://digital-research.academy/) (DRA) share a vision of a research culture that is more open, transparent, collaborative, inclusive, reproducible and sustainable.

We believe that improving research is not just about developing better tools, technologies or policies. 
It is fundamentally about empowering the people who do research, supporting the communities around them, and creating the conditions in which good research practices can thrive.

The Open Science Retreat brings together researchers, open science advocates, research support professionals, trainers and others who are passionate about improving research. 
It is a space to connect, exchange ideas, collaborate and reflect on how we can create systemic change. 
Part unconference, part hackathon, part networking event and part retreat, it offers something often missing from academic life: the time and space to step away from everyday responsibilities, think creatively, build relationships and recharge.

An important part of our philosophy is recognising that research is done by people. 
Those working to reform research culture are often doing so alongside demanding jobs, limited resources and sometimes in isolation. 
We therefore believe that supporting the people behind research is just as important as improving research itself. 
Community building, peer learning, mentorship, rest and genuine human connection are not extras; they are essential ingredients for sustainable change.

The Digital Research Academy complements this by making the skills and knowledge needed for high-quality research more accessible. 
We build a network of excellent trainers and provide training in three interconnected areas: Open Science, Data Literacy and Research Software Engineering. 
We also invest in a supportive and sustainable trainer community, helping experts share their knowledge, develop their teaching and receive fair compensation for their work.

Together, our mission is to bring people, skills and communities together to make better research possible. 
We want to create spaces where ideas flourish, connections turn into collaborations, skills translate into practice, and people feel supported and empowered to drive meaningful change in the research ecosystem.

### What did the Open Science Retreat (and DRA) achieve during your fiscal sponsorship with OLS?

Some of the key achievements during this period include:

* We managed to host as a pre-event a hackathon for the UKRN Open Research Across Disciplines resource (blogpost here: [https\://www\.ukrn.org/2026/06/15/reflections-from-the-open-science-retreat-2026/](https://www.ukrn.org/2026/06/15/reflections-from-the-open-science-retreat-2026/))  
* Growing the retreat internationally: We had previously organised global editions in Germany (2023), the Netherlands (2024), Switzerland (2025) and now the UK (2026)\! Having done it in the UK, we now know of an excellent venue in Wales for potential future editions\!  
* Creating space for collective action: This retreat enabled participants to develop ideas, initiate collaborations, explore systemic challenges in research and work together on concrete outputs. This year’s topics spanned collaborative work on systems thinking and mapping the open science ecosystem, as well as creative approaches to imagining the future of open research. Check out the documented outputs here: [doi:10.53962/vwam-tx4m](https://doi.org/10.53962/vwam-tx4m)  
* Strengthening the wider open research ecosystem: We developed collaborations with organisations, communities and initiatives working towards more transparent, reproducible and inclusive research. 

Beyond the tangible outputs, I think one of our most important achievements has been again demonstrating that there is a real need for spaces that bring together people working on research reform, while also recognising their need for connection, inspiration and rest.

We have helped create a community where people can feel less isolated, find collaborators, exchange experiences and return to their work with renewed energy.

### How did fiscal sponsorship help you achieve those aims?

Fiscal sponsorship helped by:

* Providing an organisational framework for receiving and managing funding & payments in the UK.  
* Supporting financial administration, budgeting and payments for that funding.
